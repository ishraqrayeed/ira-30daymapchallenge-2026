A map that keeps unfolding

Borges imagined a map as large as its territory. Here, each change of scale reveals another Denver: a park system, a familiar landscape, one mapped bench.

“280+” is Denver Parks & Recreation’s published urban park figure. The separate “parks mapped” count follows this project’s GIS filters, including urban open spaces.

Park boundaries: Denver Parks & Recreation. City boundary: Denver Public Works/Survey. Interiors: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL). OSM detail is uneven; an absent symbol does not mean an absent amenity. Soccer labels appear only on pitches tagged sport=soccer.

Light basemap: OpenFreeMap / OpenMapTiles / OpenStreetMap. Buildings use mapped heights; 3 m is the display fallback. Trees and benches use illustrative 3D dimensions at their OSM coordinates. Five leafy tree forms add visual variety in a stylized autumn palette; colours do not represent observed seasonal conditions or identify untagged species. Benches face the OSM direction when provided; otherwise facing is inferred toward the nearest mapped trail within 50 m. Popups distinguish recorded and inferred facing. The decorative grass texture does not represent surveyed ground cover. Ground is drawn flat; no elevation model is used. Tilt gives depth; this is not a surveyed 3D model.
