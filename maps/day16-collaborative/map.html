A real radio measurement, made visible.

GPS L1 is transmitted at 1,575.42 MHz. Motion along a receiver’s line of sight shifts the received frequency. These are actual D1 Doppler observations from NOAA’s CORS archive, sampled at their original timestamps once per minute.

Positive D1 means approaching; negative means receding. Every station’s convention is independently checked against range rates from the matching IGS rapid SP3 orbit. A ±100 Hz interval is shown in white. Receiver and satellite clocks and atmospheric effects can also affect the observable.

The documentary plays at 5× speed, taking about 6 seconds to show one observed hour. Introductory reveals hold the first epoch; the subsequent pass advances every link through the same observation timestamps. Measurements are held between recorded samples. No missing measurement is filled in.

The main map’s curved beams illustrate actual azimuths. Their lengths, curvature, glow and pulse speeds are visual storytelling. They are not satellite ground tracks or radio-wave propagation speeds. The pass illustration shows actual SP3-derived geocentric positions and a fixed Earth-based receiver. Earth and orbit use the same length scale; the satellite icon and motion arrow are enlarged. Orbit positions are interpolated for smooth motion; Doppler labels and graph dots use actual measured epochs.

Station selection uses geographic spacing among files with actual D1 observations. Elevation mask: 10°. This is an archived sample, not a live or complete national network. RF review is pending; this is not a positioning-accuracy product.
