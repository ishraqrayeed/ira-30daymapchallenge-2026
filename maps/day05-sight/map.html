STORY

The goal stays the same size. Its opening changes with every shooting position. This map reconstructs the horizontal angle between the posts from each recorded Messi shot at the 2026 World Cup.

The three highlighted attempts show the widest opening, narrowest opening, and longest-distance goal. All other attempts remain visible.

DATA

35 source-recorded attempts, eight matches and appearances, eight goals. Two match penalties are shown at their shared position; no shootout kicks occur in the selected events.

Coordinates and events: Noah Bair’s public 2026 event repository, credited there to WhoScored. Counts and recorded minute distributions agree match by match with Hesham El Alamy’s FIFA-report-derived attempt tables. Source clock times are displayed verbatim and may differ from official goal-minute labels.

No validated xG or complete shot-end coordinates are used. Source files were retrieved 2 November 2026 and pinned by revision and checksum. Separate raw event downloads are not provided; the source repository does not declare an explicit reuse license.

METHOD

The source uses an Opta 0–100 pitch, attacking increasing x in every period. Pitch landmarks are mapped piecewise to 105 × 68 metres using the published mplsoccer Opta/custom definitions. This preserves the penalty spot at 11 m, rather than stretching it to 12.1 m.

Goalposts: (105, 30.34) and (105, 37.66). Goal width: 7.32 m. Distance is measured to (105, 34). The angle is acos((L−S)·(R−S) / (|L−S||R−S|)), in degrees.

Both axes use the same scale. Medians and hero selection use 33 non-penalty attempts. The source’s attempt definition is retained, including the attempt categorized as a referee event in the FIFA-derived table. Outcomes retain provider distinctions where supported.

LIMITATIONS

This is the geometric angle subtended by the goalposts, not Messi’s literal visual field. Defenders, the goalkeeper, body orientation, ball control, pressure and occlusion are absent. Angle alone does not explain shot quality.

Event coordinates have finite precision; the standardized pitch may differ from actual stadium dimensions. Two attempts coincide at the penalty spot. Source definitions and clock labels differ between providers. The narrowest recorded attempt has a referee-event classification in the cross-check source.

Public access does not establish an open-data license for the event coordinates. The source attribution is retained; no open-data license is claimed for the event coordinates.
